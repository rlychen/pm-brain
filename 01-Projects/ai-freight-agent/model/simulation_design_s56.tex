\section{The bug, and the bug in my first fix}

\textbf{Today.} Capacity is defined as a multiple of the book:
\[
S \;=\; \tau \cdot n_{\text{hawbs}} \cdot \mathbb{E}[\mathrm{cw}],
\qquad
\text{per\_arc} \;=\; \operatorname{round}\!\Big(\tfrac{(1-\text{contracted})\, S_{\ell}}{|\text{arcs}_{\ell}|}\Big).
\]
No aircraft, no allotment, no payload ever enters the calculation. Two consequences:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
  \item Per-flight capacity is an \emph{artifact of schedule size}. More flights $\Rightarrow$ thinner
        slivers. Hence arcs of 24--197\,kg against a 500\,kg median shipment.
  \item $\tau$ \emph{cannot bind}. If $\text{capacity} \equiv \tau \times \text{demand}$, a tightness dial is
        definitionally inert --- which is exactly what S45 discovered empirically, sixteen sessions after
        the line was written.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{My first fix repeated the disease one layer down:}
$\text{capacity} := \text{positions} \times \text{flights}$. Capacity still scaled with the timetable.
Worse, my own numbers gave $\tau \approx 3.1$ --- \emph{slacker} than today's $\tau_{\text{eff}} \approx 1.5$
--- and would have reproduced S45's null result. Three of my assertions (\src{S2} no-dead-flights,
\src{S3} flight $\geq$ biggest-shipment, \src{S4} network $>$ one-freighter) \textbf{mechanically forced}
that oversupply, and \textbf{all of them pass on today's broken instance}. \src{S3} already exists in the
repository, green, since S36.

\textbf{The real fix.} Supply is an \textbf{exogenous, integral, per-flight allowance}; the
\textbf{schedule stays at full breadth}; tightness is dialled by \emph{how much the forwarder holds},
never by how many flights exist.

\section{Supply, demand, timeline}

\paragraph{Supply.} Per flight $f$, drawn on the supply RNG stream with a \textbf{lane-dependent mean}
(this preserves the approved S50 O--D-lane heterogeneity; an i.i.d. draw would silently revert
D-A25\,--\,D-A30):
\[
m_f(\mu) = \big\lfloor \mu + U_f \big\rfloor, \qquad
n_f(\mu) = \big\lfloor \rho\mu + U'_f \big\rfloor, \qquad
\text{cap}(f) = \text{positions} \times (1500\,\text{kg},\ 4.5\,\text{m}^3),
\]
with $U_f \sim \mathcal{U}(0,1)$ drawn \emph{once} and held fixed across every sweep cell (CRN).

\paragraph{Demand.} Four events. The phantom flight $d^{*}$ --- which anchored both reveal and commit, and
which 40--70\% of shipments could not even use --- is \textbf{deleted}, together with
\src{book\_lead\_*}, \src{tier\_coupled\_arrival} and \src{lead\_buckets}.

\section{The tightness lever}

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Sweep $\mu$ --- the mean number of positions per accessed flight. Continuous, $\mu \in [1.0, 4.0]$.}
\end{quote}

Because $m_f = \lfloor \mu + U_f \rfloor$ with $U_f$ fixed across cells, capacity is \textbf{pathwise
monotone} in $\mu$: raising $\mu$ weakly raises \emph{every} flight's capacity on \emph{every} sample path.
The arc set never changes, so routing freedom is invariant. It is a pure supply knob on its own RNG
stream, so the demand draw stays byte-identical.

\paragraph{Rejected.}
\textbf{Flight count} --- moves scarcity \emph{and} routing freedom, with opposite signs on $L_2$; a
response curve would be unattributable. \textbf{Book size} --- it sets arrivals per cycle, and at
$\approx$1 arrival/cycle \textbf{M0 $\equiv$ M1$'$ byte-identically} (measured), so it modulates whether the
estimand \emph{exists at all}.

\paragraph{Report.} $\tau_w$ and $\tau_v$ \emph{separately}; $\tau_v$ is the one that binds. $\tau$ is an
\textbf{output}, plotted against $\mu$.

\paragraph{Confounds --- state them, do not hide them.} Below $\mu = 1$ you are deleting flights, not
tightening (hence the hard floor). Low $\mu$ starves \emph{large} shipments first, so tightness changes the
\emph{composition} of who is starved --- report eligibility by shipment-size decile. And $\mu$ is
entangled with the reservation's own value by construction.

\paragraph{Two keys, not one.}
\textbf{Cost is keyed on flight departure date; service is keyed on \src{book\_at}.} Cost is
\emph{not additively separable over shipments} --- MAWB fixed fees, ULD counts, weight-break rates and the
spot envelope are all joint over riders, and that jointness \emph{is} the estimand. Flights are exogenous
and arm-invariant, so flight-keyed cost is losslessly attributable; \src{book\_at}-keyed service is
per-shipment and pairs cleanly across arms.

\paragraph{Two gaps, not one.}
\textbf{Planning gap $0.005$} (that is the policy --- a real planner also stops at a gap).
\textbf{Scoring gap $0$} (that is measurement error). $L_2$ is a difference of two gap-stopped solves, and
the band currently swallows the signal.

\section{Where the gates live}

Not in tests, and \textbf{not in a standing review agent}. The interface-seam agent is eleven sessions
overdue \emph{and}, when it did run, it \textbf{printed a zero-capacity flight and certified it} as
``exactly the intended emergent-scarcity mechanic''. A fifth reviewer is more surface, not a gate. And this
defect class is \textbf{data-dependent}: a config change produces a new invalid world with \emph{zero code
diff}, so hooks and test files see nothing.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{\src{validate\_instance()} raises inside \src{load()}} --- the single chokepoint every
        consumer passes through. No flag, no opt-out.
  \item \textbf{A round-trip check inside \src{persist()}} --- persist, re-load, compare, raise. Ten lines.
        Catches B1 and B2 the day they land, and every future field forgotten in the schema.
        \emph{Verified: zero false positives.}
  \item \textbf{Post-solve invariants raise inside \src{solve()} / \src{score\_run()}} --- per-flight
        capacity, $\src{status} = \textsc{optimal}$, no departed flight, no impossible connection.
  \item \textbf{An \src{instance\_card.md} beside every \src{scenario.db}}, diffed on every commit. The
        56-session failure was that nobody ever looked at the numbers \emph{because the numbers were never
        in front of anyone}.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Assertions must read the decayed, at-book-time catalog.} Mine read the \emph{nominal} dict
(1{,}500--4{,}500\,kg) while the world broke at \emph{effective} capacity (197\,kg median, post-decay).
That is precisely why they all passed.

\paragraph{Twelve existing tests codify the defects as contracts and will veto the fix.} Among them:
\src{assert fallback\_count > 0} (cargo \emph{must} strand), \src{assert sc.otp < 1.0} (OTP \emph{must}
fail), \src{assert real\_cost < 0.5 * total\_cost} (penalties \emph{must} dominate), and
\src{assert all(1500 <= c <= 4500 for c in spot\_wcap)} (the broken magnitude, pinned). Delete them first.
