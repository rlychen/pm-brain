## Slide 2 — Vision

# The planning brain for global freight.

Every shipment in the world is a planning decision — what mode, what carrier, what consolidation, what to do when something breaks. Today that brain is fragmented across five mode-specific tools, thirty thousand forwarders, and millions of human-hours.

**We are building one engine, rented by any forwarder, that runs the planning loop end-to-end across air, ocean, and trucking.**

*Tier-1 routing intelligence as infrastructure. We don't replace the TMS. We replace the planner's brain.*

## Slide 3 — Problem (Tuesday afternoon scene)

**The Tuesday afternoon scene** *(composite from forwarder-ops research; named operator validation in progress)*

A senior air coordinator at a $200M Tier-2 forwarder sits down at 3pm to build tomorrow's outbound MAWBs. Excel sheet open. WhatsApp threads with HKG / SHA / SIN partner offices. 47 HAWBs in pipeline. Builds three versions over two hours as cargo-readiness shifts, BSA utilization warnings fire, and DG holds drop in late. **No artifact survives the day.** Tomorrow they start over.

**The truth:** thirty thousand forwarders globally make planning decisions like this every day, across every mode. The math is harder than Excel — sequential decisions under cargo-readiness uncertainty, allotment minimums, co-loader relationships, DG/lithium constraints, and three coupled cutoffs (CFS, flight, dispatch).

**The wallet:** ops capacity equivalent to 1.5–2 senior planners per $200M forwarder for air alone. The wallet is operations headcount, not seat licenses — and it multiplies across modes.

## Slide 4 — Why now

**The infrastructure for autonomous-when-earned planning landed this year.**

1. **The stack matured in 2025.** MCP + capable LLMs (Claude 4.6+, GPT-5) + open-source MILP solvers (HiGHS, OR-Tools) + agentic frameworks (LangGraph). The same stack would have taken $20M and 5 years in 2022.

2. **Vertical AI is the proven model.** Harvey (legal AI): $50M → $195M ARR in 12 months at $11B valuation. Augment (logistics): $85M Series A. Raft: 40% of A&A Top-25 on document AI. *We are not Harvey* — different buyer, different unit economics — but the trajectory shape (AI replacing knowledge work, services-as-software pricing) is now well-established.

3. **The window is open.** DSV closed its $15.9B Schenker acquisition April 2025 — the largest tier-1 distracted for 2–3 years. CargoWise dominates 24 of A&A Top-25, which means the market wants an intelligence layer *on top* of the TMS, not a replacement.

## Slide 5 — Solution

**One engine. Multimodal. Agentic. Built for the messy parts.**

We sit above the TMS as the planning brain — ingesting shipment requests, making routing decisions across modes, and committing them autonomously *when the math is certain and the operator has earned trust.*

**Three operator surfaces, one engine:**

| Surface | What it does | Who uses it |
|---|---|---|
| **Quote desk** | RFQ → cost-to-serve quote in seconds | Front office / commercial |
| **Consolidation planner** | Pipeline → MILP-solved build plan | Network ops / coordinators |
| **Exception handler** | Disruption → replanned route + customer-aware decision | Ops on duty |

**Same engine: MILP optimizer + heuristics + ML feasibility predictor + LLM orchestration agent.** Exact math for high-stakes decisions, fast heuristics for routine cases.

**What the engine handles that Excel can't:**
- Sequential decisions under cargo-readiness uncertainty (re-plan as actuals replace estimates)
- Allotment / BSA minimums with dead-freight penalty math
- Co-loader relationship constraints (preferred / blocked masters with persistent memory)
- DG / lithium / temp-controlled eligibility (IATA TCR, section II, etc.)
- Three coupled cutoffs (CFS, flight, dispatch) with carrier-specific buffers

## Slide 8 — Why we win (REFRAMED)

**The moat is explainability + audit + earned autonomy. Plus the buy-vs-build gap.**

→ **The moat: explainable, auditable, autonomous-when-earned.**
LLM-only competitors cannot certify their answers — they need a human to approve every booking. Our engine carries a certificate of optimality, an explanation tree per decision, and an immutable audit trail. The agent commits autonomously *only when* the math is certain *and* the operator has earned trust through measured override patterns. This is what insurance, compliance, and ops directors actually require — not "fully autonomous from day one."

→ **The buy-vs-build defense.**
Tier-2 internal data teams *can* build 60–70% of this engine in 12–18 months for ~$1.5–2M of engineering time, then another team to maintain it forever. We get them to production in 16 weeks for a paid pilot. **Time-to-value, not technology novelty, is the arbitrage.** This is the real competition — not WiseTech, not Augment. It's the COO's internal DS team.

→ **The founder edge.**
Built this architecture inside tier-1 platforms — twice. **Delivered $110M+ annual savings, 82% automation, 72% cost-to-serve reduction.** We know the math, the data shape, the failure modes, the operator UX. We are packaging what only tier-1s could afford to build — and we are doing it before they license theirs out.

**Three competitive scenarios to watch:**
1. **WiseTech extends CargoWise into multimodal planning** (medium probability, 18–24mo) — *primary risk.*
2. **Augment or Raft moves up-stack into routing decisions** (medium probability, 12–18mo with their capital).
3. **A tier-2 builds internally and open-sources** (low probability, but the threat shapes our pricing).

## Slide 12 — Go-to-market

**Sequenced distribution. Buyer-defined KPI.**

| Path | Time to first customer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **GoFreight accelerator** (months 1–6) | 60–90 days | Open REST API, no partner gate, mid-size customer base |
| **CargoWise integration** (months 6–18) | **9–14 months / customer** | Partner application Q3 2026; per-customer integration via eAdaptor (schema gating + admin queue reality) |
| **Magaya / Riege** | 12+ months | After design-partner traction |

**Design partner contract — buyer-defined success metric:**
- Scope: air consolidation planner on one gateway (e.g., LAX outbound)
- Duration: 16 weeks · Floor: $25K paid pilot
- **Primary KPI: planner hours saved per build cycle vs pre-pilot baseline** (the buyer's metric, not ours)
- Secondary: co-load mix shift; cost delta vs baseline
- Go/no-go gate at week 12 · refund clause if KPI not met · production conversion at 90 days

**Trust ramp:** Co-pilot ≥4 wk → Supervised ≥8 wk → Autonomous per-lane (gated by *jointly-defined* lane-specific performance thresholds, not a fixed override rate). *Autonomy is earned, not claimed.*
