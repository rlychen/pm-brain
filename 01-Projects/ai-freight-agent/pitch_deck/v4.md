## Slide 2 — Vision

# The planning brain for global freight.

Every shipment in the world is a planning decision — what mode, what carrier, what consolidation, what to do when something breaks. Today that brain is fragmented across five mode-specific tools, thirty thousand forwarders, and millions of human-hours.

**We are building one engine, rented by any forwarder, that runs the planning loop end-to-end across air, ocean, and trucking.**

*Tier-1 routing intelligence as infrastructure. We don't replace the TMS. We replace the planner's brain.*

## Slide 3 — Problem (Tuesday afternoon scene)

**The Tuesday afternoon scene** *(composite from forwarder-ops research)*

A senior air coordinator at a $200M Tier-2 forwarder sits down at 3pm to build tomorrow's outbound MAWBs. Excel sheet open. WhatsApp threads with HKG / SHA / SIN partner offices. 47 HAWBs in pipeline. Builds three versions over two hours as late bookings arrive. The plan that ships to CFS is the third version. **No artifact survives the day.** Tomorrow they start over.

**The truth:** thirty thousand forwarders globally make planning decisions like this every day, across every mode. The planning brain is fragmented across mode-specific tools, tribal knowledge, and chat threads.

**The wedge:** ops capacity equivalent to 1.5–2 senior planners per $200M forwarder — and that's just air. Multiply by ocean LCL, ocean FCL, trucking. The wallet is operations headcount, not seat licenses.

## Slide 4 — Why now

**The infrastructure for autonomous planning landed this year.**

1. **The stack matured in 2025.** MCP + capable LLMs (Claude 4.6+, GPT-5) + open-source MILP solvers (HiGHS, OR-Tools) + agentic frameworks (LangGraph). The same stack would have taken $20M and 5 years in 2022.

2. **Vertical AI ARR ramps are proven.** Harvey (legal AI): $50M → $195M ARR in 12 months, $11B valuation March 2026. Augment (logistics agent): $85M Series A Sept 2025. Raft: 40% of A&A Top-25 on AI document processing. Services-as-software works when AI replaces knowledge work.

3. **The window is open.** DSV closed its $15.9B Schenker acquisition April 2025 — the largest tier-1 distracted for 2–3 years. CargoWise dominates 24 of A&A Top-25, which means the market wants an intelligence layer that sits *on top* of the TMS, not one that replaces it.

## Slide 5 — Solution

**One engine. Multimodal. Agentic.**

We do not replace the TMS. We sit above it as the planning brain — ingesting shipment requests, making routing decisions across modes, and committing them autonomously when the math is certain.

**Three operator surfaces, one engine:**

| Surface | What it does | Who uses it |
|---|---|---|
| **Quote desk** | RFQ → cost-to-serve quote in seconds | Front office / commercial |
| **Consolidation planner** | Pipeline → MILP-solved build plan | Network ops / coordinators |
| **Exception handler** | Disruption → replanned route + customer-aware decision | Ops on duty |

Same engine: **MILP optimizer + heuristic + ML feasibility predictor + LLM orchestration agent.** Different math per problem — exact optimization for high-stakes decisions, fast heuristics for routine cases, all coordinated by one agent.

## Slide 6 — Wedge + Roadmap

**Air consolidation is the MVP wedge. Multimodal is the long arc.**

**Why air consolidation first:**
- Hardest planning math (build-up windows, density constraints, capacity churn, transshipment timing)
- Highest labor cost per shipment
- No dominant SaaS incumbent in the consolidation planning layer
- Engine extends directly to ocean LCL (structurally the same problem)

**The five-phase build:**

| Phase | Component | Why next |
|---|---|---|
| **MVP** | Air consolidation planner | Hardest math, biggest labor unlock, no incumbent |
| **Phase 2** | Ocean LCL consolidation | Same consolidation math; immediate engine reuse |
| **Phase 3** | Ocean FCL routing | Allocation + vessel cap math; LaTeX model drafted |
| **Phase 4** | Trucking routing | Closes door-to-door |
| **Phase 5** | Intermodal stitching | Cross-mode optimization — the tier-1 capability |

## Slide 8 — Why we win

**Three points. The moat is the math layer.**

→ **Deterministic math = autonomy.**
Most AI competitors are LLMs with retrieval. Their agents need a human to approve every booking — they save minutes, not headcount. Our engine carries a certificate of optimality. The agent commits without human-in-the-loop *because the math is provable*. This is what tier-1 internal platforms do. No one else ships it as a product.

→ **Planning vs execution boundary.**
We plan (intelligence). The TMS executes. Clean scope — no overlap with incumbents, no integration ambiguity. We sit on top of CargoWise, not against it.

→ **Founder edge.**
Built this architecture inside tier-1 platforms — twice. We know the math, the data shape, the failure modes, the operator UX, the integration surface. We are packaging what only tier-1s could afford to build.

## Slide 12 — Go-to-market

**Sequenced distribution. Defined pilot terms.**

| Path | Time to first customer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **GoFreight accelerator** (months 1–6) | 60–90 days | Open REST API, no partner gate, mid-size customer base |
| **CargoWise integration** (months 6–18) | 8–12 months / customer | Partner application Q3 2026; per-customer integration 6–9 mo via eAdaptor |
| **Magaya / Riege** | 12+ months | After design-partner traction |

**Design partner contract — defined success metric:**
- Scope: air consolidation planner on one gateway (e.g., LAX outbound)
- Duration: 16 weeks · Floor: $25K (paid pilot, not free)
- KPI: planner hours saved per build cycle OR co-load mix shift OR override-rate trajectory
- Go/no-go gate at week 12 · refund clause if KPI not met · production conversion at 90 days

**Trust ramp:** Co-pilot ≥4 wk → Supervised ≥8 wk → Autonomous per-lane (gated by override rate ≤8%). *Autonomy is earned, not claimed.*
