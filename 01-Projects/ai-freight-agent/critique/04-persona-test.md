# Persona Test — does the v3 air MILP actually serve the four personas?

**Date:** 2026-05-27
**Subject:** `model/air_freight_routing.tex` v3 (O-D-arc graph; MAWB = (arc, g); fallback-arc; quadratic-tardiness PWL) + `model/air_graph_construction.md` + `architecture.md` §7.
**Persona reference:** the four DITL personas at `docs/forwarder-operations-analysis/day-in-the-life/{01..04}.md` (front office, network ops, compliance/customs, exceptions/replanning). Note: the older `personas_and_tools.md` uses a different cut (shipper / ops planner / business analyst / compliance) — where the cuts overlap (compliance = compliance; ops planner ≈ network ops) the analysis carries over; where they diverge (shipper, business analyst) I flag it.
**Anti-fabrication:** every quantitative claim is either sourced through the DITL docs (which themselves source or label `Inferred:`) or labelled `Inferred:` here.

---

## Executive summary

The v3 air MILP is built precisely for one role inside one persona: the **consolidation planner** (Persona 2 §9) doing the daily MAWB-vs-co-load decision. For that role it is the right tool — symmetry-free MAWB = (arc, g), density-mixed chargeable weight, quadratic-tardiness-with-fallback-arc, and the O-D-arc graph all map directly to the P1–P5 task sequence the DITL identified as un-instrumented. For every other role, the MILP is either upstream (it doesn't speak the role's input format), downstream (the role consumes its output but needs a translation layer the MILP itself does not provide), or orthogonal (it doesn't touch the work at all). The strongest fit is Persona 2 (the consolidation planner specifically — not the whole persona); the weakest is Persona 3 (compliance), which the MILP serves only by absorbing a few of their outputs as input parameters. Adoption risk is concentrated in two places: (a) the gap between the MILP's batch-solve mental model and the interrupt-driven reality of Personas 2/4 (the *coordinator* hats, not the planner hat), and (b) the absence of any operator-facing translation layer in the model itself — `z^{routing}`, `z^{tardiness}`, `K^{fb}` are math objects, not the "+€2,400 vs hold 24h" sentence an exception handler needs to put in front of an account manager.

## Persona 1 — Front office / commercial operations

**Roles in this persona** (DITL §2): KAM, inside sales / quote desk, customer service / coordinator, pricing / procurement.

**1. Workflow fit.** The model is a planning-time multi-shipment optimizer over a known supply catalog and a known set of HAWBs. Front-office roles either (a) don't have a HAWB set yet (KAM, BD), (b) work on one quote at a time at human conversational latency (quote desk: Wisor / cargo.one already compress this to seconds), or (c) handle status / milestone comms after the routing decision (CS coordinator). None of these is a multi-shipment cost-minimization problem. The closest fit is the **quote desk** in `mode_compare`-style use ("ocean vs air for this shipment given the deadline"), but that maps to a per-shipment solve and the front-office's clock is 15–45 min/quote intake ([Wisor](https://wisor.ai/how-freight-forwarders-win-more-rfqs/)), of which the routing optimization is the smallest sub-component — and competitors (cargo.one, WebCargo, CargoAi) already do the air-rate-shortlist at zero marginal effort.

**2. Coverage.** `Inferred:` <5% of front-office daily work would touch the MILP directly. The KAM's day is internal coordination + customer comms (DITL §4.1, §5.1: dominant share); the quote desk's day is intake + rate lookup (≥50% per DITL §5.2 sources); CS is templated notifications + EDI gap-fill; pricing/procurement is rate-card maintenance + carrier negotiation. The MILP doesn't show up in any of these. Where it could show up — single-shipment "best air route given my deadline" called from the quote desk — the value-add over an aggregator's rate-sort is incremental (the MILP adds correct MAWB economics + consolidation potential), but the consolidation benefit is invisible on a one-shipment quote.

**3. Missing for this persona.**
- **Quote-engine surface.** Sell rate and margin are explicitly out of scope per §Problem Statement and `architecture.md` §11 — the MILP outputs *cost only*. The front office does not buy a routing tool; it buys a quote engine.
- **Customer-facing explanation.** "Here is why your shipment goes via FRA with a 1-day deconsol dwell" needs to be a paragraph, not a `z^{routing}` decomposition.
- **Rate freshness signal.** Front-office trust depends on knowing whether the underlying rate is contract (binding) vs spot snapshot (stale) vs TACT fallback. That layer lives in `architecture.md` §8 but is not surfaced in the MILP output.
- **Win-back / re-quote after expiry.** Quotes go stale in days; the model has no notion of quote validity.

**4. Over-engineered for this persona.** Quadratic tardiness PWL, group partition logic, MAWB break-selection binaries, IATA next-break-down disaggregation, fallback-arc semantics — front-office personas need none of this exposed. For a single-quote use, an LP / shortest-path with a per-HAWB CW lookup would deliver 95% of the answer at 5% of the cognitive surface. The MAWB structure is overkill when there's exactly one HAWB.

**5. Adoption risk (week 1).** The KAM and quote desk will reject the tool in week 1 if it doesn't beat cargo.one's air-rate shortlist on speed, and beats it only at multi-shipment scale (which the quote desk doesn't have). The CS coordinator will never see it. The pricing/procurement persona could in principle use the MILP's `z^{routing}` for BSA utilization analytics — but that's a P2 reporting use, not a daily workflow tool. If the team positions the MILP as a quote-desk tool to win Persona 1 quickly, it will lose because (a) it solves the wrong problem (cost, not quote turnaround) and (b) it sits in a saturated AI-vendor space (`architecture.md` §12: "Not a quote-desk vendor"). Don't pitch this to Persona 1 as a daily tool.

## Persona 2 — Network operations

**Roles in this persona** (DITL §2): air export coordinator, ocean export coordinator, drayage dispatcher, CFS supervisor, **consolidation planner**.

**1. Workflow fit.** Sharply bimodal. For the **consolidation planner** (DITL §9), the model is a near-perfect fit: P1 eligibility filter ↔ per-shipment subgraph pre-filter (§prefilter); P2 MAWB-vs-co-load economics ↔ MAWB-arc vs co-load arc in the graph with density-mixed `CW_{a,g}`; P3 cutoff-vs-ready optimization ↔ effective cutoff stack folded into `CO_a^*` + C.9; P4 hub-vs-direct ↔ deconsol-dwell arc cost vs through-arc cost; P5 compatibility-group selection ↔ the `g(k)` partition (DGR / temp / VAL singletons). The afternoon decision block (DITL §3, §4.5) is exactly the batch-solve cadence the MILP serves. For every other Persona 2 role, the model is upstream of their work (coordinators *execute* against a published plan; CFS supervisors *build* against a published sheet; the dispatcher's day is terminal portals and chassis pools — none of which the air MILP touches).

**2. Coverage.** For the consolidation planner: `Inferred:` 60–80% of the 2–4 hr afternoon decision block (DITL §3, §4.5). The remaining 20–40% is WhatsApp validation with origin offices and coordinator hand-off — comms work that no MILP solves. For coordinators / CFS supervisors / dispatchers: `Inferred:` <2% of their day touches the MILP directly. They consume its output (the build sheet, the per-MAWB tender plan) — but the consumption is unilateral; they don't invoke the optimizer.

**3. Missing for this persona.**
- **Plan-to-execution handoff.** The model outputs `x_{k,a}`, `z_{a,g}`, `CW_{a,g}` and three cost numbers. The CFS supervisor needs a build sheet (per-MAWB ULD type, contour, HAWB-piece list, screening status, DG segregation). The air export coordinator needs booking instructions per MAWB. None of these is in the model — they sit downstream and need a "Route Formatter" component (`personas_and_tools.md` master tool inventory) that doesn't yet exist.
- **WhatsApp / partner-office validation loop.** DITL §6.5: the planner's plan is validated in WhatsApp threads with origin offices for cargo-ready confirmation. The MILP assumes `t_k^{rdy,early}` / `t_k^{rdy,late}` as inputs; the planner's actual workflow is iteratively re-soliciting these from origin agents. No tool surface for that.
- **Mid-week re-solve on rate updates.** DITL §9.3 Pain 3: "a new master-loader rate sheet arrives mid-week … no way to re-evaluate the prior week's plans counterfactually." The MILP can re-solve, but the architecture doesn't define what triggers it or how the planner sees the delta.
- **Late-pipeline shipment incremental insertion.** DITL §9.3 Pain 1: high-value HAWB arrives 4 hr before cutoff. The MILP's batch-solve nature has no incremental-insertion semantics; a full re-solve over the bucket is overkill at that latency and the planner won't wait.
- **Sensitivity / "what's binding"** explanation. The DITL §9.4 explicitly names "which constraints are binding? which customers' constraints are costing the most?" as MILP-grade value. The §Output and Diagnostics block reports `z^{routing}` / `z^{tardiness}` / `z^{fallback}` and the fallback set — that's a feasibility / cost summary, not a binding-constraint or shadow-price readout.

**4. Over-engineered for this persona.** For the consolidation planner specifically, not much is over-engineered — every constraint family maps to a real operational fact (DGR / temp / screening, cutoff stack, lithium PI gating, embargo, allotment). The one place where the MVP front-loads complexity that the planner won't use in week 1: the **five-layer carrier policy cascade** (§Carrier Policy and Rules Resolution) is more elaborate than what mid-size planners do today (DITL §9.2: "mental model of which customers will tolerate hub routing"). Worth keeping for v2 but the MVP could ship with a simpler `(blacklist, allow-list)` pair and add layers as override patterns demand.

**5. Adoption risk (week 1).** The consolidation planner will reject the tool in week 1 if (a) the solve takes longer than 5–10 minutes on their full pipeline (the afternoon decision block is finite — DITL §3 calls out the planner's window as materially shorter than the operational window); (b) the output is the four cost numbers and no plain-language plan they can paste into a WhatsApp thread or coordinator email; (c) the recommended plan deviates from their tribal-knowledge expectation without an explanation they can defend to the origin office. Risk (c) is the killer — DITL §9.2: "a mental model of which customers will tolerate hub routing vs which insist on direct" is *not encoded* in the rules engine and will show up as overrides on day one. The 5-tier carrier rule cascade has the right *shape* to absorb these — but only after several weeks of override-log feedback.

## Persona 3 — Compliance, customs, documentation

**Roles in this persona** (DITL §2): LCB, entry writer, export coordinator (ISF/AMS/ICS2/ACI), DG operator, HS classification specialist, documentation specialist.

**1. Workflow fit.** Near-zero direct fit. The compliance persona's day is governed by external hard clocks — vessel/flight load times, 24h-pre-load ISF, foreign customs windows (DITL §3, §4.3) — and the work is *attestation* on a per-shipment / per-HAWB basis (Form 7501 attestation is non-delegable; DGD pre-acceptance dialogue is human-to-human; HBL/MBL pairing is one-by-one). The MILP runs batch-cadence, optimizes cost, and treats compliance as a *predicate* (lithium_ok, embargo_ok, cargo_type_ok in §prefilter) — not as a workflow. The MILP doesn't help an entry writer file an entry; it doesn't help an LCB attest a Form 7501; it doesn't help a DG operator dialogue with the airline DG officer.

**2. Coverage.** `Inferred:` <2% of compliance daily work touches the MILP directly. The cases where it does: the export coordinator confirming that the planned routing's POL cutoff (`CO_a^*` includes AMS / ICS2 / ACI per §graph-construction) is achievable given their filing capacity. That's a one-way data flow, not an interactive tool.

**3. Missing for this persona.**
- **Customs hold probability into the model.** DITL §10 corrected the parent doc: CBP physical exam ~3–5%; "any hold" including PGA ~15% ([USA Customs Clearance](https://usacustomsclearance.com/process/cargo-examinations/), [GAO](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-22-104210.pdf)). The model carries `δ^{cust}_k` as a deterministic per-HAWB customs dwell — fine for MVP, but compliance will rightly note the actual distribution is bimodal (clean release in minutes vs PGA hold in 5–7+ business days, DITL §10 reality-check). The Phase 4 probabilistic transit-time hook (§deferred item 1) acknowledges this; the MILP doesn't help compliance plan around it today.
- **Document / data readiness as a constraint.** The model assumes the data exists at solve time (HS code `hs_k`, lithium spec, screening status). DITL §3 + §4: the compliance reality is that a meaningful share of incoming shipments arrive with missing/incomplete shipper paperwork — emergency telephone missing on DGD, FDA prior notice not yet filed, valuation worksheet missing on a related-party transaction. The model doesn't have a "shipment is route-blocked pending docs" state.
- **PGA-touched batch carve-out.** DITL §4.2: FDA / EPA / FCC / FWS-touched entries follow different release windows. The model treats customs as a single `δ^{cust}_k` scalar — no PGA distinction.
- **CF-28 / CF-29 reactive load (§7.2 of Persona 3 DITL).** Has no place in the model. Reasonably out of scope.

**4. Over-engineered for this persona.** Nothing in the MILP is over-engineered for compliance — most of it is invisible to them. The **lithium battery taxonomy and per-flight acceptance** section is the one part that touches their world directly, and the DGR / PI / section / Wh structure mirrors how the DG operator already classifies (DITL §4.4). The risk is the opposite of over-engineering: under-coverage of what compliance considers structural (PGA holds, screening chain-of-custody re-verification on cross-IAC handoff per DITL §4.4, valuables-cage segregation that today's tribal-knowledge mode catches DGR 95%+ but misses occasionally per DITL §9.3 Pain 2).

**5. Adoption risk (week 1).** Compliance won't reject the tool because compliance won't *use* the tool — they'll be on the receiving end of "this MILP plan needs an ISF by 04:00 CST tomorrow, can you make it?" The risk is that the MILP recommends a routing that compliance flags as un-filable (carrier with bad PGA history at this lane, lithium PI that this airline's DG desk rejects), the override rate spikes on those shipments, and the rules engine has to absorb the patterns. That's a v2 problem, but compliance should be in the design-partner loop for the rule overlay early so they recognize their constraints in the cascade rather than only catching errors after the fact.

## Persona 4 — Exceptions, track & trace, re-planning

**Roles in this persona** (DITL §2): track & trace clerk, control tower operator (where it exists), exception handler, customer service for disruptions, claims handler. Per memory `project_core_user_reality.md`: **Persona 4 ≈ Persona 2 at mid-size** — same humans wear planning hat and reactive-mode hat.

**1. Workflow fit.** Moderate fit, and the most architecturally interesting one. The exception handler's canonical event (DITL §4.2: CX cancellation cascade for 8 HAWBs) is exactly the case the MILP was rewritten for — the fallback-arc design (§3.4) and the post-solve fallback set (`K^{fb}`) replace solver-INFEASIBLE with a structured per-HAWB rescue signal. That's a real fit. The MILP can re-solve the subgraph of affected HAWBs with the cancelled flight removed and return a recovery plan. Where the fit fragments: the handler's day is comms-heavy (~20% of day on inbound carrier/partner comms, DITL §5.3) and decision-heavy (~40–70%) — the MILP serves only the decision step, and only after a human (or the LLM agent layer) has classified the inbound signal as material and mapped it to the right subgraph of HAWBs.

**2. Coverage.** `Inferred:` 10–25% of the exception handler's day on a normal day (the re-plan decision step itself); rises on major-event days when one disruption hits dozens of HAWBs and the MILP re-solves the affected book. The track & trace clerk, CSR-for-disruptions, and claims handler get 0% direct coverage — their work is comms / doc-assembly / status, not optimization.

**3. Missing for this persona.**
- **Trigger and scope.** The model assumes "you've decided to re-solve and you've handed me the affected HAWB set." DITL §9.1: the actual re-plan decision time is mostly *not* the solve — it's the materiality assessment ("is this disruption big enough to warrant a re-plan?") and the scope choice ("which HAWBs are affected?"). `architecture.md` §7.2 capability #3 explicitly puts materiality assessment in the LLM agent layer — so the MILP is correctly silent on it, but the agent layer's design is the load-bearing piece for Persona 4.
- **Trade-off explanation.** DITL §4.2 narrative: the handler's most time-consuming step was calling the account manager and explaining "rebook on LH at +€2.40/kg (~€2,800 extra), or accept 24h delay." The MILP produces `z^{routing}` and `z^{tardiness}` per solve; the *delta* between the pre-disruption plan and the recovery plan needs differencing logic + sentence generation that lives in `architecture.md` §7.2 capability #4. Without it, the handler is re-deriving the explanation from the math each time.
- **Customer-comms generation.** DITL §4.4: "draft email with new ETA, D&D forecast, ask whether to accelerate drayage." Out of MILP scope, rightly — but the system needs to deliver it.
- **Multi-event coalescing.** Major-event days (Red Sea, port labor action) batch dozens of events; the MILP can absorb the batch but the architecture currently treats re-solve as a single-shipment trigger (`architecture.md` §5 re-plan triggers).
- **Approval-chain awareness.** DITL §9.4: re-plan approval depends on dollar premium ($1K / $1K–$10K / $10K+). The MILP returns a number; the system needs to route that number to the right approver.

**4. Over-engineered for this persona.** Less so than for Persona 1. The fallback-arc design is squarely *right* for Persona 4 (the "rescue signal" semantics in §3.4 map to DITL §4.2 reality). The five-layer rule cascade may be over-engineered for emergency re-routing where the handler often *intentionally* breaks the rule cascade — "I know we never use this carrier on this lane but they're the only one with capacity in the next 12 hours." There should be a per-solve "override layer" that the rules engine accepts without removing the constraint from the catalog. That's a v1 ergonomic, not a model fix.

**5. Adoption risk (week 1).** The exception handler will reject the tool in week 1 if (a) the re-solve takes more than 2–3 minutes on the affected subgraph (DITL §4.2 timeline: handler had 8 HAWBs + 12-hour window — that's a 2-hour budget for decision + commit, of which solve time should be seconds-to-tens-of-seconds); (b) the MILP rejects a plan the handler knows is feasible (rule-cascade false-positive); (c) the output is the four cost numbers and not "LH alternative at +€2,400, ETA holds; CX next-day at +€0, ETA +24h." Risk (c) is the same week-1 killer as Persona 2. The good news: the §Output and Diagnostics structured fallback set and per-HAWB tardiness flag taxonomy is the right *raw material* for an agent-layer translation, and `architecture.md` §7.2 names the capability. The gap is whether the translation layer ships in MVP or as v2.

## Cross-persona gaps and adjacencies

### Gaps that span personas

**Gap 1 — Operator-facing translation of MILP output.** Personas 2 and 4 both need (a) plain-language plan summaries, (b) per-shipment "why this routing" explanations, and (c) deltas between candidate plans. The model gives the math; no component in `architecture.md` §7 owns the translation. Capability #4 in §7.2 ("re-plan trade-off explanation") names it for re-plans only; the planning case needs the same surface. **This is the single most cross-persona-load-bearing missing capability.**

**Gap 2 — Multi-shipment plan as a deliverable.** Persona 2 (planner) outputs build sheets to coordinators / CFS; Persona 4 (handler) outputs revised tender lists to coordinators + customer comms. Both need the MILP solution serialized into formats the receiving role consumes. The "Route Formatter" tool exists in `personas_and_tools.md` master inventory but is unscoped in the architecture and not in the model.

**Gap 3 — Iterative shipper / origin-office data refresh.** DITL §6.5 (planner) + §4.4 (DG operator) + §4.6 (doc desk) all describe iterative WhatsApp / email loops to refresh inputs (cargo-ready windows, lithium SoC, missing emergency telephone, COO from producer). The MILP treats these as solve-time-fixed parameters. Personas 2 and 3 will both demand a "shipment is data-blocked" surface or the MILP will optimize on stale inputs. This is the unstructured-channel-wedge piece (memory `project_unstructured_channel_wedge.md`) showing up at the optimizer interface.

**Gap 4 — Sensitivity / shadow-price / binding-constraint readout.** Personas 2 (planner — DITL §9.4 explicitly names it) and Persona 2's pricing/procurement role (which the team's older `personas_and_tools.md` calls the BA persona; B5–B7 in that doc) both need "what's binding? which customer's constraint is costing the most? what would change if we relaxed X?" The §Output and Diagnostics section reports cost decomposition but not duals. This is a small additive deliverable on top of the existing solve and should be in MVP — without it, the planner cannot defend the plan to procurement.

**Gap 5 — Customs hold uncertainty.** Persona 3 will flag the deterministic `δ^{cust}_k` as wrong in week 1 (DITL §10 + §4.2). The Phase 4 probabilistic transit-time deferred item covers it on paper; in practice the gap will surface as overrides every time the model commits a plan that compliance knows is exam-prone for that consignee × commodity × port. The override-log + rules-engine feedback loop is the right long-run mechanism (`architecture.md` §9), but compliance should be in design-partner loop early.

**Gap 6 — Rate freshness as a first-class signal.** Persona 1 (pricing/procurement) and Persona 2 (planner — DITL §9.3 Pain 3) both care which rates are contract vs spot snapshot vs TACT vs predicted. `architecture.md` §8 has the 5-tier strategy; the MILP body says nothing about source/freshness. At minimum, the output should carry per-arc rate-source metadata so the operator can see "the recommended plan depends on a TACT fallback on the FRA→XYZ leg — this is non-binding."

### Adjacent surface area — what the MILP pushes into other components

The MILP's choices commit the system to several adjacent capabilities that the personas will then expect from the integrated product. None of these is wrong; all of them are unwritten today.

- **Rules engine.** The five-layer carrier-policy cascade (§Carrier Policy) is correct in shape but needs (i) an authored ruleset per tenant at onboarding (Persona 2 + Persona 3 deliverable, not a developer task); (ii) a debugger surface — "why did this carrier get excluded?" — for Persona 2 overrides and Persona 4 emergency overrides; (iii) override-driven learning per `architecture.md` §9.
- **Transit-time service.** §6 C.6 binds against `transit(k, a)` which is deterministic in MVP. Personas 2 and 4 both need P85/P90 quantile binding (deferred item 1) — without it, the model commits to mean times and Persona 4's reactive load scales linearly with the prediction error.
- **Agent layer materiality + re-plan trigger.** `architecture.md` §7.2 capability #3 is load-bearing for Persona 4 — but its decision boundary is currently a flag taxonomy in agent_design.md, not an algorithm. The MILP gives the agent good raw material (the `K^{fb}` rescue signal, the tardiness flag) but the trigger logic is unspecified.
- **Quote engine / cost-to-price layer.** The MILP cleanly says "cost only." `architecture.md` §11 has a "quote desk" surface at L1. For Persona 1 to ever touch the system, this layer has to exist.
- **Operator console.** The plan inbox + flag triage in `architecture.md` §7.3 is the surface every persona ultimately interacts through. Its design is more load-bearing than the MILP for adoption — if the console doesn't surface `z^{tardiness}` and `K^{fb}` in a way Persona 2's afternoon decision block can act on in 5 minutes, the MILP doesn't matter.
- **Build-sheet / tender-instruction generator.** Persona 2's coordinators and CFS supervisors need the MILP's `x_{k,a}` and `z_{a,g}` rendered into a per-MAWB build sheet (CFS supervisor) and per-MAWB tender instruction (coordinator). Today this is a `personas_and_tools.md` future tool with no component owner.

### Where the integrated system stops being the MILP

The DITL roll-up §6 frames the operational Pareto as "3% of shipments / 40% of org time" — the planning side of which is Persona 2's consolidation planner and the reactive side of which is Persona 4. The MILP serves the planning side natively and the reactive side after wrap. The other 60% of the day — quote-desk routine (Persona 1), routine customs filings (Persona 3), milestone notifications (Persona 1 CS, Persona 4 T&T) — sits in Buckets A and B of the workflow-vs-AI framework (`architecture.md` §15) and is rightly someone else's problem to solve. The MILP being silent on those is correct, not a gap.

## Primary user reality check

Per memory `project_core_user_reality.md`: the primary user is "senior coordinator with budget influence, wearing the consolidation-planner hat 2–4 hr/day" — not a dedicated optimization analyst. The DITL §9.5 sources this independently: at mid-size forwarders the consolidation planner role is owned by a senior air export coordinator (~50% time) or network ops manager (~20–30% time), often the same person who owns BSA allocation. No BLS occupation maps cleanly. Tool stack today is Excel + WhatsApp + customer-relationship knowledge — the thinnest of the 18 roles surveyed (DITL §6.5).

**How well does the v3 MILP serve this user?**

**Strong fit on the substance.** Every load-bearing element of the model maps to a real decision in the consolidation planner's afternoon block: MAWB = (arc, g) is the actual commercial object they pick a tender for; density-mixed `CW_{a,g}` is the economics that justify consolidation (DITL §4.5 + §9 references the Dimerco 15–30% co-load savings claim as the central trade-off, vendor-sourced upper bound); the cutoff stack into `CO_a^*` is the P3 cutoff-vs-ready optimization; the five rule layers are the customer × lane × commodity rules they hold in their head today; the fallback arc is the "this shipment is unplannable, escalate" signal they generate manually today via spreadsheet flagging. The architecture got the math right.

**Weak fit on the ergonomics.** The user is *not* an analyst. They are an interrupt-bursty senior coordinator (DITL §3 cluster B: "bursty interrupt") wearing the planner hat for 2–4 hours after spending the morning on bookings + cutoff cascades. They need the MILP to: (a) load fast (their pipeline of 60–120 HAWBs is the worst case, DITL §4.5); (b) accept inputs from a TMS report they already export to Excel; (c) hand them a plan they can defend to the origin office in WhatsApp and to the coordinators in email; (d) let them override individual line items without invalidating the whole plan; (e) remember the override reasons so the rule cascade learns. The model body is silent on (a)–(e); these are correctly the orchestrator's job per `architecture.md` §7 but the architecture has not committed to specifications for them either.

**The risk if the team mis-targets.** The v3 model reads — accurately — like it was designed for a "dedicated planner" persona that does not exist at mid-size. If the design-partner forwarder rolls it out to their actual planner (the senior coordinator wearing the hat) without the orchestrator wrap, the user will reject it in week 1 not because the math is wrong but because the model speaks a language built for the persona that the memory record warns against. The fix is not in the model — the model is right. The fix is treating the orchestrator + console + rule-author + override-log surfaces as MVP, not as v2.
